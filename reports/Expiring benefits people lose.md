# Young workers forfeit thousands through retirement fine print

The biggest deadline-bound money that Expire Valley does not yet track is in **employer retirement and equity plans**, not coupons or perks. These include the 401(k) match left unclaimed (historically **1 in 4 participants, about $1,336 a year each**), unvested employer contributions lost when someone changes jobs (in **30% of job separations, averaging 40% of the departing worker's balance**), and vested stock options that lapse in the standard **90-day post-termination window** (**about 50,000 workers walked away from more than $1.8B in 2022**). Next comes **forfeited PTO**: 46% of workers leave paid time off unused, and each lost day is worth about salary ÷ 260. Students face a separate set of one-shot losses: about **$1.2B in federal refunds** goes unclaimed each year after the 3-year window closes, and the high-school class of 2024 left **$4.4B in Pell Grants** on the table by not filing the FAFSA. A long tail of email-borne deadlines (flight credits, hotel certificates, gift and promo cards, return windows, settlement claims) is small per item but nearly universal, and an inbox pipeline can extract it cheaply. Two findings should shape the roadmap. First, a single **job-change event** triggers at least six separate clocks: vesting, option exercise, commuter run-out, COBRA, PTO payout and sign-on clawback. Second, three documents, the **Summary Plan Description, the pay stub and the equity grant agreement**, hold most of the high-value fields. The recommended next additions are 401(k) match and vesting, PTO, equity windows, dependent care FSA, unclaimed tax refunds, travel credits and certificates, student-aid deadlines, and employer education and EAP budgets.

## Retirement and equity plans hold five-figure losses that recur at every job change

The **401(k) match** is the single most valuable benefit people fail to collect. A Financial Engines study of 4.4 million participants found that **1 in 4 missed some or all of their match, averaging $1,336 a year and about $24B nationally**, with younger and lower-income employees most likely to miss out ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2015/05/12/yees-missing-out-on-24b-of-free-money.html)). That study is from 2015. Auto-enrollment has since raised participation to **94% versus 64% under voluntary enrollment** ([NAPA on Vanguard How America Saves 2026](https://www.napa-net.org/news/2026/6/plan-design-drives-record-retirement-participation-better-outcomes-vanguard/)), so outright non-participation is shrinking. Under-contribution persists, though. Vanguard's newer recordkeeping data shows **only 22% of workers earning under $30K maximized their match, versus 80% of those earning $150K+** ([Vanguard](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/corporatesite/us/en/corp/articles/new-401k-match-formulas-can-unlock-retirement-gains.html)). The loss accrues every pay period rather than at one deadline. The rescuing action is to raise the deferral percentage to the match cap. A subtler variant is the missing "true-up": a plan that matches per paycheck forfeits match for the rest of the year if the worker front-loads contributions. The researchers found no prevalence data on true-ups, but the SPD states whether a plan has one. An extraction pipeline needs the match formula from the **Summary Plan Description (SPD)** or benefits guide, and the deferral rate from the **pay stub**.

**Vesting forfeiture** is the most underappreciated loss for job-hopping young professionals. Vanguard's analysis of 4.7 million separations found that **forfeitures occur in 30% of job separations and average 40% of the affected participants' final balances**. Losses concentrate among lower-income workers, and **only 33% of participants could correctly say whether their plan even has a vesting schedule** ([Vanguard via MN LCPR](https://www.lcpr.mn.gov/vestingWG/7.28.26/Vanguard%20article%20does_401k_vesting_help_retain_workers%202.2025.pdf); [NAPA](https://www.napa-net.org/news/2025/2/do-401k-vesting-schedules-help-with-worker-retention/)). About **49% of plans vest immediately**, so the risk applies to roughly half of workers. For them, a cliff date is a hard deadline. Take a new grad earning $80K with a 4% match who leaves one month before a 3-year cliff: they forfeit roughly $9,600 plus growth. The rescue is to know the vest date before resigning and, where possible, time the departure after it. The data lives in the SPD (vesting schedule, any last-day or 1,000-hour rule) and in the **recordkeeper statement** (Fidelity, Vanguard, Empower), which shows the vested balance.

**Stock options** produce the largest single-event losses. Most startups give **90 days after termination to exercise**, and **just under 50,000 workers walked away from vested options worth more than $1.8B in 2022** on a 409A basis, which works out to roughly $36,000 of paper value per person ([Carta](https://carta.com/data/linkedin-employee-stock-options-90-days-too-short/)). In Q1 2024 only **20% of terminated grants had a window longer than 90 days**, and ISOs exercised after 90 days lose their ISO tax status ([Carta](https://carta.com/learn/equity/leaving-company/post-termination-exercise-period/)). Some of these lapses are deliberate. Exercising costs cash, can trigger tax, and the paper value can prove illusory, so the product should frame this as a decision with a deadline rather than a guaranteed loss. The source documents are the **option grant agreement** and the equity portal (Carta, Shareworks, E*TRADE, Schwab).

**Employee stock purchase plans (ESPPs)** are the clearest money left untouched by people who never enroll. **85% of qualified ESPPs offer a 15% discount and 83% offer a lookback**, yet **65% of companies report that fewer than half of employees participate** ([NASPP](https://www.naspp.com/blog/five-trends-in-espps)). A 15% discount alone implies at least a 17.6% gain on contributions if shares are sold at purchase. Enrollment opens only before each offering period, typically every six months, so missing the window costs half a year. The pipeline needs the offering-period dates from the **ESPP enrollment notice** or plan prospectus. Smaller in dollars but also employer-funded is the **HSA employer seed**, which averaged **$727 among contributing accountholders** ([PlanAdviser/EBRI](https://www.planadviser.com/new-milestone-majority-of-hsa-assets-invested/)). It is lost only when the worker fails to enroll in the HDHP, open the account, or complete a required wellness step. Seed money appears in W-2 Box 12 code W and on Form 5498-SA. Prior-year HSA contributions remain allowed until the tax-filing deadline ([IRS Pub 969](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969)).

## Paid time off and employer budgets reset on predictable calendars

**PTO** is the most widespread employer benefit people lose. Pew found that **46% of workers with paid time off take less than they are offered**, and managers, salaried and college-educated workers are more likely to leave time unused ([Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/08/10/more-than-4-in-10-u-s-workers-dont-take-all-their-paid-time-off/)). The most-cited loss figure is dated: in 2018, **768 million days went unused and 236 million were forfeited outright, worth $65.5B** ([Hotel-Online on U.S. Travel](https://www.hotel-online.com/news/study-a-record-768-million-u-s-vacation-days-went-unused-in-18-opportunity-cost-in-the-billions)). So roughly 31% of unused days were truly lost, and the rest rolled over or were paid out. Location changes the risk. **California bans use-it-or-lose-it**, permits accrual caps (often about 1.75× the annual accrual), and requires payout at termination ([Nolo](https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/california-rules-vacation-paid-time-off.html)). Colorado, Montana and Nebraska reportedly follow similar rules ([Shouse Law](https://www.shouselaw.com/ca/blog/use-it-or-lose-it-vacation-policy/)). A California user's risk is therefore hitting the accrual cap, which halts accrual, rather than a December 31 forfeiture. At $80K a year, one day is worth about $308. The pipeline reads the accrued balance from the **pay stub or HRIS** (Workday, ADP, Rippling, Gusto) and the reset date, carryover cap and state from the **employee handbook PTO policy**. Floating holidays and sabbatical windows follow the same pattern, but the researchers found no usage data on either.

The **dependent care FSA** is the newly enlarged blind spot. The limit rose to **$7,500 for 2026, from the $5,000 it had been since 1986**, but only where the employer amended its plan ([Polsinelli](https://www.polsinelli.com/publications/new-year-new-dependent-care-fsa-limits)). A DCFSA may offer a 2.5-month grace period but **cannot use the health-FSA carryover**, so any unused balance is forfeited outright ([Paylocity](https://paylocity.com/resources/tax-compliance/alerts/tax-alert-unused-dependent-care-fsa-funds)). No DCFSA-specific forfeiture rate was found. The closest analogue is the health FSA, where **about half of holders forfeited an average of $441 in 2022** ([EBRI](https://www.ebri.org/content/full/new-analysis-of-3.2-million-flexible-spending-accounts-finds-average-contributions-increasing-while-half-forfeiting-funds-to-their-employers)). A related gap sits next to Expire Valley's existing FSA tracker: the **run-out period**. This employer-set window, often about 90 days, is for submitting claims for expenses already incurred. Missing it forfeits money even when the expenses were incurred on time ([HR Daily Advisor](https://hrdailyadvisor.com/?p=27918)). EBRI also found that workers who manually submitted at least one claim forfeited **$114 less** on average ([EBRI](https://www.ebri.org/crawler/view/new--fast-fact--research-report-finds-workers-who-manually-submitted-at-least-one-flexible-spending-account-expense-had-fewer-forfeitures--saved-average-of--114)). Prompting claim submission therefore measurably works. DCFSA balances and run-out dates come from the **FSA administrator statement** (HealthEquity/WageWorks, WEX, Optum, Navia) and the **SPD or Section 125 plan document**.

**Commuter benefits** carry a termination trap. Pre-tax transit and parking are each capped at **$340 a month in 2026** ([Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/irs-releases-annual-increases-to-health-fsa-and-transportation-fringe-benefit-limits-for-2026)). Unused balances cannot be refunded in cash after termination and are forfeited once the run-out ends ([Newfront](https://newfront.com/blog/commuter-benefits-upon-termination-employment-2)). One university plan allows 60 days and a stored-value card plan allows 90 ([UPenn](https://cms.business-services.upenn.edu/transportation/contact-us/faqs/commuter-faqs/35-commuter-passes/adp-commuter-check-card/360-i-have-taken-another-position-and-have-left-penn-how-do-i-claim-my-unused-credits.html)). Because elections can change monthly, the rescue is to lower the election ahead of a known departure.

**Employer-funded budgets** split into two groups: well-used and almost ignored. Lifestyle spending accounts average **$850 per employee per year with 89% utilization** in Compt's customer base ([Compt](https://compt.io/blog/stipend-and-lifestyle-spending-account-benchmarks/)). That is vendor data, and it suggests modest breakage. **Tuition reimbursement** is the opposite case. **57% of organizations offer it** ([PlanSponsor](https://www.plansponsor.com/irs-updates-faq-on-section-127-educational-assistance-programs/)), yet median participation among Training magazine's Top 125 applicants is just **3.75%** ([Training magazine](https://trainingmag.com/?p=9753)), against a tax-free limit of **$5,250 a year** that OBBBA made permanent and will index after 2026 ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/127)). **EAP counseling** follows the same pattern. Plans typically grant **6–8 free sessions per issue per year** ([ConocoPhillips](https://hr.conocophillips.com/current-employees/employee-assistance-plan-eap/); [Carle](https://benefits.carle.org/well-being/resolutions)), while vendor-reported utilization runs about **3–6% of employees** ([Spill](https://www.spill.chat/us/mental-health-statistics/eap-statistics)). For both, non-use is not always a loss, since many workers do not need a degree or counseling in a given year. Still, these are the highest-value benefits that workers least often know they have. The source is the **benefits guide or total-rewards statement**, which also lists **vision allowances**, typically **$120–$150 for frames on a 12- or 24-month cycle** ([CalHR 2026 VSP summary](https://benefits.calhr.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/362/2025/12/2026-VSP-Member-Benefits-Summary.pdf)), and **wellness incentives**. Employers spent about $600–$750 per employee on wellness incentives in the last benchmarks found, though no current neutral data exists ([BenefitsPRO](https://benefitspro.com/2015/03/27/wellness-incentive-spending-sets-record/?amp=1)).

## Tax and student-aid deadlines create the largest one-shot losses for students

The **3-year federal refund window** fits Expire Valley's student audience almost perfectly. The IRS estimated **$1.2B in unclaimed tax-year-2022 refunds owed to more than 1.3 million people, with a median refund of $686**, forfeited to the Treasury after April 15, 2026 ([CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/03/23/irs-owes-1-3-million-americans-about-686-each-deadline-to-claim-is-near/180074/)). Most unclaimed refunds belong to people who never filed, often students and part-time workers below the filing threshold whose employers still withheld tax ([Fox 9](https://www.fox9.com/news/2022-tax-refund-april-15-deadline-looms)). The next window to close is tax year 2023, around April 15, 2027. A pipeline can flag it from a **W-2 with Box 2 withholding** combined with no filed return, confirmable through the **IRS wage-and-income transcript**. The **EITC** is a related loss: about **1 in 5 eligible taxpayers don't claim it** ([CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2025/02/03/the-biggest-tax-credit-many-people-miss-the-eitc/155391/)), leaving roughly **$7.3B a year** unclaimed ([TIGTA](https://www.tigta.gov/sites/default/files/reports/2024-11/2018IER004fr.pdf)). Its value skews toward filers with children, so it is less central to a single-student audience. Two smaller look-back windows exist. Residential energy credits ended after 2025 ([IRS FS-2025-05](https://www.irs.gov/EnergyCreditFAQs)), but 2025 installs can still be claimed. Prior-year IRA contributions are allowed up to the filing deadline, and the 2026 limit is $7,500 ([Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/irs-ups-limits-for-401k-and-ira-contributions-in-2026)).

**Student aid** has the largest aggregate loss of any student-specific item. NCAN estimated that the high-school **class of 2024 left $4.4B in Pell Grants unclaimed**, because about **830,000 Pell-eligible students didn't complete the FAFSA** ([NASFAA](https://www.nasfaa.org/news-item/36182/NCAN_Unclaimed_Pell_Grants_Totaled_4_4_Billion_for_the_High_School_Class_of_2024)). That averages over $5,000 per student. State grants enforce hard dates. The **Cal Grant 2027–28 deadline is March 2, 2027** (the FAFSA and CADAA opened October 1, 2026) ([USC Financial Aid](https://financialaid.usc.edu/undergraduates/prospective/dates-and-deadlines.html)), and CSAC reportedly offers no extension for late filers ([CollegeReadyParent](https://collegereadyparent.org/downloads/CollegeReadyParent-California-Cheat-Sheet.pdf)). Two other rules affect continuing students. Pell carries a **600% lifetime cap**, about 12 full-time semesters ([Wichita State](https://wichita.edu/administration/financial_aid/pell_leu.php)). From 2026–27, OBBBA also removes Pell when non-federal grants cover the full cost of attendance ([Oregon State](https://financialaid.oregonstate.edu/legislative-updates)), so an outside scholarship can trigger a revised award letter worth watching. The source documents are the **FAFSA Submission Summary, the state aid portal and the school award letter**, plus studentaid.gov for Pell lifetime eligibility used.

For recent graduates, **student loan transitions** create deadline risk rather than direct forfeiture. A court vacated SAVE in March 2026. Servicers began notifying SAVE borrowers on July 1, 2026, and **borrowers who don't choose a plan within 90 days are auto-placed** in another plan ([studentaid.gov](https://studentaid.gov/articles/save/)). The new RAP sets payments at 1–10% of income ([ED fact sheet](https://www.ed.gov/about/news/press-release/fact-sheet-trump-administration-simplifying-student-loan-repayment)). For low-income students, public benefits renewals carry similar procedural risk. **Medicaid expansion adults move to 6-month redeterminations on January 1, 2027** ([Georgetown CCF](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/03/06/cms-releases-guidance-on-6-month-medicaid-renewals-for-expansion-adults/)), and SNAP time limits now reach ages 18–64 ([Hamilton Project](https://www.hamiltonproject.org/publication/paper/a-primer-on-snap-work-requirements/)). Both depend on answering an agency notice by its due date. These matter, but they come from servicer and state-agency notices that vary widely, so they are harder to extract than the items above.

## Email-borne consumer deadlines are small per item but nearly universal

**Airline flight credits** are the largest travel pool that Expire Valley's card-credit module does not cover. Most expire **12 months from the original ticket date, not the cancellation date**. Some programs require only booking by that date, others require travel to be completed. Southwest dropped its no-expiry policy for bookings made on or after May 28, 2025 (Basic fares 6 months, Choice fares 12) ([Autopilot](https://withautopilot.com/blog/airline-flight-credits-compared)). **Hotel free-night certificates** from co-brand cards generally expire 12 months after issue. Marriott ended extensions in December 2023 ([Frequent Miler](https://frequentmiler.com/marriott-rules-for-extending-free-nights/)), and Hyatt requires checkout before the expiry date ([Frequent Miler](https://frequentmiler.com/when-do-hyatt-certs-expire-perhaps-a-day-earlier-than-you-think/)). **Points expiration** now affects a narrower set of programs: American (24 months of inactivity), Frontier (12), IHG (12), Choice and Wyndham (18), and Marriott, Hilton and Hyatt (24). Delta, United, Southwest, JetBlue and Alaska no longer expire miles ([Frequent Miler](https://frequentmiler.com/how-to-keep-points-and-miles-alive/)). Even so, **45% of travel cardholders say they have let points or miles expire** ([BestMoney](https://www.bestmoney.com/credit-cards/learn-more/travel-credit-card-rewards-survey)). All three items are extractable from **cancellation-confirmation emails, card renewal emails and loyalty account statements**.

**Gift cards and store credit** are the most common item by headcount. Bankrate found that **43% of adults hold at least one unused gift card, averaging $244 and about $27B in total** ([Bankrate](https://bankrate.com/f/102997/x/c53caa68d4/gift-card-survey-press-release.pdf)), and **25% have let a gift card expire** ([Digital Transactions](https://www.digitaltransactions.net/survey-americans-have-21-billion-in-unused-gift-card-balances/)). Federal law guarantees at least 5 years for gift card funds, but **promotional and loyalty cards are exempt** ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/bcreg20100323a.htm)). The short-fuse risk therefore sits in "bonus" promo cards rather than purchased cards. **Return windows** are best defined around the holidays: for 2025 purchases, Amazon's deadline was January 31, 2026 and Best Buy's January 15, with up to 15% restocking fees on opened electronics ([About Amazon](https://aboutamazon.com/news/retail/amazon-holiday-return-policy-2025); [Money Talks News](https://www.moneytalksnews.com/retailers-with-extended-policies-for-the-holiday-season/)). **Free-trial conversions** became a bigger risk after the FTC's click-to-cancel rule was **vacated by the 8th Circuit in July 2025** ([Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftc-click-to-cancel-rule-days-before-compliance-deadline)). Student perks such as Spotify Premium Student auto-convert to full price when re-verification fails after graduation ([Spotify Support](https://support.spotify.com/article/premium-student/)).

Claims-based money has the lowest redemption of anything studied. The FTC found a **median claims rate of 9% in consumer class settlements** ([Ballard Spahr](https://ballardspahr.com/insights/alerts-and-articles/2019/10/ftc-study-class-action-settlement-notices-have-room-to-improve)), but payouts are small: about **$29 per claimant** in the Facebook $725M settlement ([NBC Philadelphia](https://www.nbcphiladelphia.com/news/national-international/725m-facebook-settlement-payments-have-started-to-go-out-when-can-you-expect-yours/4268081/)). For mail-in rebates, **forgetting to submit is the top reason for non-redemption, at 41%** ([CMSWire](https://www.cmswire.com/cms/customer-experience/mailin-rebates-annoying-customers-one-upc-code-at-a-time-024746.php)). **Unclaimed property** is the largest pool, at about $70B held by states against $4.49B returned in FY2024 ([Fox LA](https://www.foxla.com/news/theres-billion-dollars-unclaimed-money-us-check-see-youre-owed-money)). It never expires, though, so it fits a one-time "find your money" feature better than a deadline tracker.

## Recommended shortlist: eight additions and the documents they need

The shortlist favors items that score high on value × frequency and that come from documents the user can upload once a year. It also folds adjacent items into Expire Valley's existing modules where that is cheaper than building new ones.

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 401(k) match capture and vesting cliff | Match formula, true-up, vesting schedule, last-day rule, deferral %, vested balance, hire date | Summary Plan Description; pay stub; recordkeeper quarterly statement |
| 2 | PTO forfeiture and accrual cap | Accrued balance, reset date, carryover cap, payout rule, work state | Pay stub or HRIS export; employee handbook PTO policy |

A low-cost **inbox layer** for gift and promo cards, return windows, trial conversions and settlement claim deadlines should run alongside these. Each item is small, but the parsing pattern (an order, sign-up or notice email with a date) is uniform, and the ninth through seventeenth rows of the ranking mostly come from email.

## Bank and card statements alone surface about 14 deadline signals

Users can upload a bank or card statement most easily, but it shows only transactions, not plan rules. A statement can therefore *detect* that a deadline exists (a charge, a credit, a fee, a balance), and the pipeline has to supply the deadline from known rules or a follow-up document. The research notes support only a few rows. The card-credits research names monthly statements as the place to find posted statement-credit lines ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/money/credit-cards/articles/how-the-american-express-platinum-card-can-pay-for-its-annual-fee-3-times-over-in-2026)). EBRI found that manual FSA claims cut forfeiture by $114 ([EBRI](https://www.ebri.org/crawler/view/new--fast-fact--research-report-finds-workers-who-manually-submitted-at-least-one-flexible-spending-account-expense-had-fewer-forfeitures--saved-average-of--114)). Holiday return deadlines are documented ([About Amazon](https://aboutamazon.com/news/retail/amazon-holiday-return-policy-2025)), and so are points inactivity rules ([Frequent Miler](https://frequentmiler.com/how-to-keep-points-and-miles-alive/)). Rows marked **GK** rest on general knowledge, were not cited in the research, and need verification before they ship as product rules.

| Signal | What the statement shows | Deadline | Rescuing action | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Subscriptions and trials | Recurring same-merchant charges; a $0 or $1 trial charge | Next billing date (inferred from cadence) | Cancel or downgrade before renewal | Partly cited: no federal click-to-cancel rule after the 2025 vacatur ([Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftc-click-to-cancel-rule-days-before-compliance-deadline)); cadence inference is GK |
| Annual card fee | "Annual membership fee" line | ~30 days after posting to get a refund on cancel or downgrade | Request a retention offer, downgrade or cancel | GK |
| Coupon credits | Statement-credit lines posted (or absent) this period | End of month, quarter, half-year or calendar year | Use the credit, or confirm enrollment | Cited ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/money/credit-cards/articles/how-the-american-express-platinum-card-can-pay-for-its-annual-fee-3-times-over-in-2026)); extends the existing card module |
| 0% intro APR / deferred interest | Promo balance and promo end date, often printed | Promo expiry; deferred-interest plans charge back-interest on any remaining balance | Pay off or transfer before expiry | GK |
| Rotating 5% categories | Base-rate earn on category spend (not on the statement, but the issuer is) | Quarterly activation, usually before or early in the quarter | Activate each quarter | GK |
| Bank account bonus | New account, bonus pending, direct-deposit lines | Direct-deposit or balance deadline; minimum days open before closing without clawback | Meet the requirement; hold the account open past the clawback period | GK |
| Overdraft / maintenance fees | Fee lines | Usually short (bank-specific) | Request a courtesy refund; meet waiver conditions | GK |
| Billing errors / unauthorized charges | Duplicate, unknown or wrong-amount charges | 60 days from statement (Fair Credit Billing Act for cards; Reg E for debit/EFT, with shorter windows for some liability tiers) | File a dispute in writing or in the app | GK |
| Purchase / price protection | Recent large purchase on an eligible card | Typically 90–120 days from purchase | Claim if damaged, stolen or price-dropped | GK (the notes flag card protection as unresearched) |
| Retailer returns | Recent purchase at a major retailer | Retailer window (e.g., Amazon holiday returns until Jan 31, 2026) | Return before the date | Cited ([About Amazon](https://aboutamazon.com/news/retail/amazon-holiday-return-policy-2025)); standard windows GK |
| FSA/HSA-eligible charges | Pharmacy, clinic, dental or vision charges paid with a personal card | FSA plan-year end or run-out; HSA has no deadline | Submit a reimbursement claim with receipt | Cited ([EBRI](https://www.ebri.org/crawler/view/new--fast-fact--research-report-finds-workers-who-manually-submitted-at-least-one-flexible-spending-account-expense-had-fewer-forfeitures--saved-average-of--114); [HR Daily Advisor](https://hrdailyadvisor.com/?p=27918)) |
| Insurance renewals | Annual or step-up premium to an auto, renters or home insurer | Renewal date (inferred from charge cadence) | Re-shop 2–4 weeks before renewal | GK |
| Points balances | Rewards balance; co-brand statements sometimes print expiry | Program inactivity rules (e.g., AA 24 months, IHG 12) | Earn or redeem to reset the clock | Cited ([Frequent Miler](https://frequentmiler.com/how-to-keep-points-and-miles-alive/)) |
| Idle cash | Large checking or savings balance, tiny interest line | None (an ongoing opportunity cost, not a deadline) | Move it to a higher-yield account | GK |

Statements are therefore a strong **trigger layer**: the cited rows (coupon credits, FSA-eligible charges, returns, points) map directly onto benefits in this report. They are a weak **rules layer**. Fourteen signals need only one upload, but eight of them depend on GK deadlines that vary by issuer or bank, and the product should present those as "check by" prompts rather than firm dates.

## Conclusion

The research reframes what "expiring benefits" means for this audience. The coupon-style perks that dominate consumer attention are real but small. The large losses come from **employment documents nobody re-reads after onboarding**: an SPD vesting table, a grant agreement's 90-day clause, a handbook carryover cap. They also cluster around two predictable moments that Expire Valley can treat as first-class events. A **job change** opens a bundle of clocks at once: vesting, option exercise, commuter run-out, COBRA's 60-day election ([SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/ResourcesAndTools/hr-topics/benefits/Pages/COBRA-election-and-payment-periods.aspx)), PTO payout and sign-on clawbacks, which are typically prorated over 12 months ([SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001196501/000104746915004227/a2224512zex-10_48.htm)). The **January–April window** stacks PTO resets, FSA grace and run-out dates, the Cal Grant deadline, prior-year IRA and HSA contributions, and the 3-year refund cutoff. A "leaving my job" checklist and a "new year deadlines" digest would likely capture more dollars than any single new benefit type.

The evidence has gaps. No study measures breakage on monthly card credits, DCFSA forfeiture or flight-credit expiry. The 401(k) missed-match figure is a decade old, and the PTO forfeiture total dates from 2018. Several items (true-up prevalence, HIPAA special-enrollment windows, student loan grace periods) rest on standard rules the researchers could not re-verify. Expire Valley has an opportunity here: once users upload these documents, it would hold the first real dataset on how often each deadline is actually missed. Publishing that data could become a distribution advantage in its own right.
