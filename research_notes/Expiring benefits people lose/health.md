# Health "use it or lose it" and deadline-bound benefits (US, 2026)

Scope: health benefits beyond the ones already tracked (health FSA Dec 31 forfeit, dental annual max reset, deductible/OOP reset). Each entry gives what expires, cadence, typical value, how often it's lost, the rescue action, and the source document an extraction pipeline should read. Research date: 2026-10-24.

## Tax-advantaged accounts: HSA, DCFSA, LPFSA, FSA grace/carryover/run-out, HRA

### Takeaway
2026 limits: health FSA $3,400 with a max carryover of $680 (Rev. Proc. 2025-32); HSA $4,400 self / $8,750 family; dependent care FSA raised to $7,500 (from $5,000) by OBBBA. About half of FSA holders forfeit money each year, roughly $441 on average (EBRI, 2022 data). An HSA never forfeits. Its deadline risk is a missed contribution window (Apr 15 of the following year) and unclaimed employer seed/match money. A DCFSA can have a grace period but no carryover, so its unused balance is simply lost.

### Cited Findings
**Health FSA 2026 parameters**
- 2026 health FSA salary-reduction limit is $3,400 (up from $3,300). Max carryover is $680 (up from $660), which is 20% of the limit. Employers may set a lower carryover cap. Each spouse with their own FSA has a separate $3,400 limit. — [Hylant](https://hylant.com/insights/blog/irs-releases-health-fsa-limits-for-2026); [IMA](https://imacorp.com/insights/compliance-2026-health-fsa-qualified-transportation-limits); [Woodruff Sawyer](https://woodruffsawyer.com/node/14981)
- Grace period (optional plan feature) lets participants spend leftover funds for up to 2.5 months after plan year end, which is March 15 for calendar-year plans. A plan generally offers either a grace period or a carryover, not both. — [Gerald explainer](https://joingerald.com/learn/financial-wellness/fsa-grace-period-explained); [Beancount 2026 guide](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/26/fsa-open-enrollment-carryover-grace-period-run-out-deadline-guide)
- Run-out period is a separate, employer-set window to *submit claims* for expenses already incurred in the prior plan year. It is typically about 90 days (often to ~March 31) but varies, and federal law requires neither a run-out nor a grace period. Missing the run-out forfeits money even when the expenses were incurred in time. — [HR Daily Advisor](https://hrdailyadvisor.com/?p=27918) (older article; verify per plan)

**FSA forfeiture statistics**
- EBRI's analysis of 3.2M FSA accounts found roughly half of FSA holders forfeited funds in 2022. The average forfeiture was $441 (up from $339 in 2019), about $1.4B across the accounts studied. About one-third of accounts in the database were pure use-it-or-lose-it designs. — [InvestmentNews summarizing EBRI](https://investmentnews.com/industry-news/half-of-fsa-holders-forfeited-funds-to-their-employers-ebri-report/253246); [EBRI release](https://www.ebri.org/content/full/new-analysis-of-3.2-million-flexible-spending-accounts-finds-average-contributions-increasing-while-half-forfeiting-funds-to-their-employers)
- Workers who manually submitted at least one FSA claim were 13 percentage points less likely to forfeit, and forfeited $114 less on average, than workers with only auto-substantiated (debit card) claims. — [EBRI fast fact](https://www.ebri.org/crawler/view/new--fast-fact--research-report-finds-workers-who-manually-submitted-at-least-one-flexible-spending-account-expense-had-fewer-forfeitures--saved-average-of--114); [BenefitsPRO](https://www.benefitspro.com/2025/01/06/workers-with-manually-substantiated-fsa-claims-less-likely-to-forfeit-funds-analysis-finds)
- Older secondary figure: 40% of FSA owners forfeited some funds (2020 study), with average forfeiture of $339–$408. The original study was not verified. — [search summary of secondary sources; treat as weak]

**Dependent care FSA (DCFSA)**
- 2026 DCFSA limit is $7,500 per household for single or married-filing-jointly filers (up from $5,000) and $3,750 for married filing separately. — [Kent State / IRS summary](https://www.kent.edu/node?page=861); [ALPA 2026 limits](https://www.alpa.org/articles/2026/01/2026-irs-contribution-and-benefit-limits); [GoodRx](https://www.goodrx.com/insurance/fsa-hsa/fsa-limits-2026)
- A DCFSA may adopt the 2.5-month grace period (by plan amendment) but cannot use the health-FSA carryover. Unused DCFSA funds are forfeited and cannot be refunded to the employee. — [Paylocity tax alert](https://paylocity.com/resources/tax-compliance/alerts/tax-alert-unused-dependent-care-fsa-funds); [LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/what-is-the-irs-use-it-or-lose-it-rule/)

**Limited-purpose FSA (LPFSA)**
- Same $3,400 limit and the same carryover/grace rules as a health FSA (it is a health FSA restricted to dental and vision so the holder stays HSA-eligible). — inferred from the [Hylant](https://hylant.com/insights/blog/irs-releases-health-fsa-limits-for-2026) health FSA limit; no dedicated LPFSA source was fetched.

**HSA**
- 2026 HSA limits are $4,400 self-only and $8,750 family. 2025 limits were $4,300 / $8,550. — [GoodRx](https://www.goodrx.com/insurance/fsa-hsa/fsa-limits-2026); [ALPA](https://www.alpa.org/articles/2026/01/2026-irs-contribution-and-benefit-limits); [Mercer 2025](https://www.mercer.com/en-us/insights/law-and-policy/2025-hsa-hdhp-and-excepted-benefit-hra-figures-set/)
- Contribution deadline for a tax year is the tax-filing due date (about April 15 of the following year). The age-55+ catch-up is $1,000 (statutory, not indexed). The balance never forfeits and rolls over indefinitely. — [IRS Pub 969](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969)
- OBBBA (effective 2026, IRS Notice 2026-05, Dec 9, 2025) has three changes:
  - Bronze and catastrophic plans count as HDHPs from Jan 1, 2026, which newly makes many marketplace enrollees HSA-eligible.
  - Pre-deductible telehealth is permanently allowed.
  - Direct primary care fees up to $150/mo individual or $300/mo family are compatible with an HSA and payable from it.
  - Sources: [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/node/153161); [NATP](https://www.natptax.com/news-insights/blog/new-rules-for-hsa-eligibility-and-tax-benefits/); [Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/irs-gives-guidance-on-hsas-under-obbba)
- Average employer HSA contribution was $727 in 2024 (EBRI, averaged across all accountholders), down because fewer employers contributed. — [TrueMed summarizing EBRI](https://truemed.com/blog/hsa-statistics) (secondary)
- PSCA survey (about 600 employers, 2024 data): three-quarters of employers contribute to employee HSAs, and 54.9% give a set amount per coverage tier. — [NAPA-Net/PSCA](https://www.napa-net.org/news/2025/9/psca-finds-more-employees-boost-hsa-contributions-invest-assets/)
- 33% of covered workers are in an HDHP with a savings option (KFF 2025). Only 3% of HSA-qualified HDHP enrollees get an employer contribution at least equal to their single deductible, vs 33% for HDHP+HRA. — [KFF EHBS 2025](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/2025-employer-health-benefits-survey/)

**HRA**
- HRA balances are employer-funded. Rollover vs forfeit is set by the employer plan, and funds are typically lost at job separation unless the plan says otherwise. — [gap: no primary source fetched; plan-dependent]

### Inferences
- The highest-dollar "lose it" events among accounts are DCFSA balances (up to $7,500 in 2026, newly raised, so families may over-elect in the first year), health FSA/LPFSA balances above the $680 carryover, and missed run-out claim submissions.
- For HSAs the loss is opportunity cost: unclaimed employer match or seed money (often conditioned on enrolling or completing wellness steps by a deadline) and a missed prior-year contribution before Apr 15. Newly HSA-eligible bronze/catastrophic marketplace enrollees in 2026 are a large group likely unaware of this.
- Where to extract the data:
  - FSA/DCFSA elections and balances: the plan administrator portal or year-end balance statement (HealthEquity/WageWorks, WEX, Fidelity, Optum, Navia). Pay stub deductions show the election amount.
  - Grace, carryover and run-out rules: the employer benefits guide, Summary Plan Description, or Section 125 cafeteria plan document.
  - HSA contributions: Form 5498-SA (contributions, filed by about May 31) and the W-2 Box 12 code W (employer plus pre-tax contributions). Employer seed/match terms are in the benefits guide.
  - HRA: the HRA plan document or portal balance.

### Gaps
- No 2024/2025 EBRI update on FSA forfeiture rates was found; $441 / ~50% is 2022 data.
- No DCFSA-specific forfeiture statistic was found.
- KFF 2025 average employer HSA/HRA dollar contributions are in Section 8/11 tables that weren't reached. The $727 EBRI figure is secondary.
- No authoritative statistic on run-out-deadline losses was found.

## Vision, hearing, dental cleanings, preventive care, and Medicare Advantage allowances

### Takeaway
Vision benefits reset every 12 or 24 months (frames often $120–$150+, contacts $105–$150+). Preventive services ($0 under the ACA) and dental cleanings (usually 2/yr) are use-it-per-period benefits. The biggest documented underuse is in Medicare Advantage: about 66% of 2026 individual MA plans offer OTC allowances (94% of SNPs), and an industry study estimated only about 30% of OTC allowance dollars are used (~$400 average per enrollee per year; ~$5B unused). Often the allowances expire quarterly or monthly.

### Cited Findings
**Vision**
- VSP employer plans show frame allowances of $120 in 2026 (Costco/Walmart equivalents $70–$90), a 24-month frame frequency, and $150 with 12/24-month frequency in a 2025 version. Some plans offer $250. Elective contact allowances are commonly $105–$150 (up to $200+), and contacts are usually in place of glasses, not in addition. Many plans run on a calendar-year cycle for exam, lenses and contacts. — [VSP Basic (Aerospace)](https://employees.aerospace.org/files/2025/11/VSP-Basic.pdf); [VSP premium (Aerospace)](https://employees.aerospace.org/files/2024/11/VSP-premium.pdf); [CalHR 2026 VSP summary](https://benefits.calhr.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/362/2025/12/2026-VSP-Member-Benefits-Summary.pdf); [CSUMB 2026 VSP summary](https://csumb.edu/media/csumb/section-editors/human-resources/up-documents/2026-VSP-Vision-Plan-Summary.pdf)
- 99% of 2026 individual MA plans include vision, 98% dental, and 98% hearing. — [KFF MA 2026 Spotlight](https://www.kff.org/medicare/medicare-advantage-2026-spotlight-a-first-look-at-plan-premiums-and-benefits/)

**Medicare Advantage OTC / flex cards / SSBCI**
- Share of plans offering an OTC allowance: 66% of 2026 individual MA plans (down from 73% in 2025) and 94% of SNPs.
- Fitness: 93% of individual plans (86% of SNPs). Acupuncture: 34% (SNPs 28%). Meals: 57% (SNPs 66%). Medical transportation: 24% (SNPs 67%).
- Part B premium reduction: 32% of individual plans offer one. Among those, 36% give $100 or more per month. The 2026 Part B premium is $202.90.
- SSBCI benefits (food/produce, utilities, pest control) are often loaded onto a flex/spending card. The allowance "may expire monthly or roll over until year-end" depending on plan.
- The VBID model ended after the 2025 plan year.
- Source for all of the above: [KFF MA 2026 Spotlight](https://www.kff.org/medicare/medicare-advantage-2026-spotlight-a-first-look-at-plan-premiums-and-benefits/)
- CHPA 2021 study: average OTC allowance about $400 per enrollee per year ($7.1B total). Utilization is about 30%, leaving nearly $5B unused. CHPA is an OTC-industry trade group, so the figures carry a commercial interest. — [CHPA](https://www.chpa.org/news/2021/11/new-study-shows-medicare-advantage-otc-benefits-programs-help-enrollees-and-health); [CHPA full report PDF](https://chpa.org/sites/default/files/media/docs/2021-10/MA%20OTC%20Full%20Report.pdf)
- A Kenvue spokesperson repeated "70% of OTC allowances go unused each year" and said OTC allowances are use-it-or-lose-it under many plans. — [The Healthy](https://www.thehealthy.com/healthcare/news-medicare-otc-benefit/); [Newsweek](https://newsweek.com/medicare-users-leaving-billions-of-dollars-benefits-unused-11111825) (citing a Milliman estimate that ~87% of MA plans offer some OTC benefit)
- Consumer-guide ranges for individual plans are about $50–$300+ per quarter (anecdotal). — [Paul B Insurance](https://www.paulbinsurance.com/medicare-advantage-plans-with-over-the-counter-otc-benefits-in-2026/)

**Preventive care / Medicare Annual Wellness Visit**
- 37.3% of beneficiaries aged 65+ received an AWV in 2019 (8.8M-person study). MA enrollees were 4.4 percentage points more likely than Traditional Medicare enrollees. — [PMC12761961](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12761961/)
- Another study put AWV uptake at only 18% of FFS beneficiaries and 25% of MA enrollees (2019). In 2015, 18% of Part B beneficiaries used an AWV. — [ADvancing States brief](https://advancingstates.org/sites/default/files/Policy-Spotlight-Annual-Wellness-Visit-508.pdf); [The Hospitalist](https://blogs.the-hospitalist.org/content/medicare-part-b-wellness-visits-1-million-2015)
- AWV uptake is lower for Hispanic MA enrollees (18.3%) than for White MA enrollees (26.3%) (2015). — [AARP PPI](https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/health/coverage-access/annual-wellness-visits-among-medicare-advantage-enrollees/)

### Inferences
- MA flex/OTC cards are the most acute "expiring" health dollars for seniors. Quarterly allowances (e.g., $50–$150/quarter) often expire on Mar 31, Jun 30, Sep 30 and Dec 31 with no rollover. A missed quarter is lost permanently.
- MA plans also often tie "member rewards" (gift cards of ~$25–$100) to completing an AWV, mammogram or colonoscopy by Dec 31. This is common practice but no source was fetched to confirm it.
- Where to extract the data:
  - Vision allowances and frequency: the vision plan Summary of Benefits / benefits guide. Last-service dates are in the vision EOB or the VSP/EyeMed portal. The pipeline needs the last-use date plus the frequency (12 vs 24 months, calendar year vs rolling) to compute eligibility.
  - Dental cleanings (2 per calendar year or 1 per 6 months): the dental Summary of Benefits and dental EOB service dates.
  - MA allowances and cadence: the MA plan's Evidence of Coverage (EOC) and Summary of Benefits (SB). Balances are in the flex/OTC card portal (e.g., NationsBenefits, OTC Network, Healthy Benefits+).
  - MA hearing aid allowance (often per ear, every 1–3 years): the EOC.
  - Preventive services at $0: the SBC "preventive care/screening/immunization" row and EOB $0 claims.

### Gaps
- No independent post-2021 OTC utilization data; CHPA 2021 is still the main source. No KFF/CMS average OTC dollar amount for 2026.
- MA hearing aid allowance amounts were not found; typical plan designs were not verified.
- No sourced statistic on how many adults skip their annual physical or second dental cleaning, or on vision-allowance non-use.

## Visit caps (PT, chiropractic, acupuncture, mental health) and EAP free counseling

### Takeaway
Commercial plans commonly cap chiropractic and acupuncture at about 20 visits per year each, often combined, and some at 12. PT is often capped at 20–30 visits (not confirmed in sources found). EAPs typically give 3–10 free counseling sessions per issue per year, with 6 and 8 most common. EAP use is low (about 3–6% of employees annually), so most of this free benefit goes unused. Mental-health parity (MHPAEA) generally prohibits stricter visit limits on therapy than on comparable medical benefits, so hard caps on therapy visits in medical plans are less common than EAP session limits.

### Cited Findings
- Providence 2023 small-group plans allow 20 chiropractic and 12 acupuncture visits per calendar year in-network. — [Providence](https://healthplans.providence.org/employers/2023/small-groups)
- Larimer County Surest plan (June 2026) has a combined 20-visit-per-plan-year limit for chiropractic plus massage, and 20 acupuncture visits per year in-network. — [Larimer County](https://www.larimer.gov/sites/default/files/surestsmm31may2026ua.pdf)
- NY Empire Plan has a 20-visit calendar-year maximum (from July 2023) for physical medicine with exceptions for network managed-physical-medicine providers. — [NY Civil Service](https://www.cs.ny.gov/employee-benefits/pa-market/shared/publications/empire-plan-report/2023/special-paep-epr-may-2023.pdf)
- Kaiser/ASH California plans cap chiropractic plus acupuncture at 20 combined per year in one version. In another, chiropractic is up to 28 and acupuncture 20, with review after 12 chiropractic visits. — [Affirm/Kaiser Chiro-Acu summary](https://benefits.affirm.com/us/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2022/08/AFRM-2022-Kaiser-ChiroAcu-Benefit-Summary-Final.pdf); [CalChoice](https://calchoice.com/getattachment/051378a5-09d2-4982-a29b-ecef02dc3ea1/Chiro_20240101.pdf)
- WHA (CalPERS) allows up to 20 medically necessary visits each for acupuncture and chiropractic per year. — [Western Health Advantage](https://westernhealth.com/calpers/plans-and-benefits/alternative-medicine)
- EAP session allowances by employer:
  - ConocoPhillips (Concern): up to 8 sessions per issue, per year, per family member. — [ConocoPhillips](https://hr.conocophillips.com/current-employees/employee-assistance-plan-eap/)
  - University of Idaho: 8 per person per issue per year. — [UIdaho](https://uidaho.edu/human-resources/benefits/core-benefits/eap)
  - Carle (Resolutions): 6 per issue per year. — [Carle](https://benefits.carle.org/well-being/resolutions)
  - Columbia (TELUS Health): 6 per topic per year. — [Columbia](https://www.cuimc.columbia.edu/node/28183)
  - Lander University: 4. — [Lander](https://lander.edu/about/offices-departments/human-resources/benefits/employee-assistance-program-eap.html)
  - Mendocino County: 10 per issue per 12 months, household members included. — [Mendocino County](https://mendocinocounty.gov/government/human-resources/employee-health-insurance/employee-assistance-program)
  - NuVantage 2020 brochure: 3. — [FDL brochure](https://about.fdlband.org/staff/downloads/2020EAPBrochure.pdf)
- Average US EAP utilization is under 10%, and typical annual utilization is about 3–6% of covered employees (vendor-sourced, and it includes non-counseling contacts). — [Spill EAP statistics](https://www.spill.chat/us/mental-health-statistics/eap-statistics)
- Everett Public Schools EAP: overall utilization 14.8% (2017), 24.1% (2016) and 19.7% (2015), but counseling cases only 3.4–5.1%. This is a single-employer example. — [Everett PS EAP report](https://docushare.everett.k12.wa.us/docushare/dsweb/Get/Document-116807/6.a.%20Quarterly%20EAP%20Report.pdf)

### Inferences
- EAP sessions are a high-value, near-zero-use benefit. At a typical therapy cash price (not sourced here), 6–8 free sessions are worth several hundred to over $1,000 per issue per year. Sessions usually reset per issue per year, and a new issue can open a fresh allotment, which users rarely know.
- Visit caps rarely "expire" in dollars, but unused visits reset at plan-year end. A user mid-course of PT or chiro near Dec 31 benefits from scheduling before the reset, especially after meeting the deductible (combined effect with deductible-met tracking).
- Where to extract the data:
  - Visit limits: the SBC (the "Limitations, Exceptions & Other Important Information" column for rehabilitation services and chiropractic care), the plan's EOC/certificate of coverage, and the benefits guide. Visit counts used are on EOBs or the insurer portal's "benefit accumulators."
  - EAP: the benefits guide or EAP flyer (vendors include ComPsych, Lyra, Spring Health, TELUS Health, Carelon, Magellan, Uprise). College students' equivalent is student health counseling session limits in the SHIP brochure.

### Gaps
- No source confirmed typical PT visit caps (often stated as 20–30/yr). No source on the share of plans with visit caps.
- No reliable industry-wide EAP utilization benchmark from a neutral source (e.g., EASNA/SHRM) was found.
- The MHPAEA point above comes from general knowledge, with no source fetched; verify before reporting.

## Wellness/gym reimbursements, wellness incentives and premium discounts

### Takeaway
Wellness incentives (HRA or biometric completion, points programs, gym reimbursements) are widespread at large employers and carry deadlines tied to the plan year or open enrollment. Employer spend averages roughly $600–$750 per employee per year on incentives, and participation is low. KFF stopped asking about wellness incentives in 2024, so current dollar benchmarks are weak.

### Cited Findings
- KFF 2025 found that 53% of large firms offer a health risk assessment, and 53% of those use incentives or penalties for completion. 43% of large firms offer biometric screening, 62% of those with incentives/penalties. 83% of large firms (56% of small) offer wellness programs (smoking cessation, weight, coaching). KFF removed wellness-incentive questions in 2024. — [KFF EHBS 2025](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/2025-employer-health-benefits-survey/)
- Average employer wellness spending was $610 per employee in 2025, down from $737 in 2024 (secondary summary of a 2025 trend report). — [Motion Connected](https://www.motionconnected.com/?p=5648) (secondary, weak)
- NBGH/Fidelity measured incentive spend at $693 per employee planned for 2015 (up from $594 in 2014). A later NBGH figure was $742. Common incentive forms are cash, gift cards and premium discounts. — [BenefitsPRO](https://benefitspro.com/2015/03/27/wellness-incentive-spending-sets-record/?amp=1); [MobiHealthNews](https://mobihealthnews.com/node/117871) ("~$700 per head ... few workers partake")
- Medicare Advantage: 93% of individual plans include a fitness benefit (e.g., SilverSneakers-type gym access). — [KFF MA 2026](https://www.kff.org/medicare/medicare-advantage-2026-spotlight-a-first-look-at-plan-premiums-and-benefits/)

### Inferences
- Typical lose-it patterns:
  - Premium surcharges (e.g., tobacco or no-HRA-completion surcharges) that are avoidable only by completing a step before an open-enrollment deadline.
  - Points/rewards programs (Virgin Pulse/Personify, Rally) whose points expire at plan-year end. Wellness HSA/HRA deposits are earned only if activities are logged by a cutoff.
  - Gym/fitness reimbursements from insurers (often ~$20/mo or $150–$400/yr) that require quarterly or semiannual submission. Typical amounts are not sourced here.
- Where to extract the data: the employer benefits guide (wellness section, surcharges), the open enrollment confirmation statement, the wellness vendor portal (points balance and deadline), pay stubs (surcharge line), and the insurer member portal (gym reimbursement form and period).

### Gaps
- No current (2025–2026) neutral benchmark for wellness incentive or premium-discount amounts, or share of employees who fail to earn them.
- No sourced data on insurer gym-reimbursement amounts or claim deadlines (e.g., the per-period submission windows of BCBS, Aetna, Cigna).
- No data on student health plan (SHIP) specific expiring benefits.
