# Employer-Provided Benefits & Perks That Expire, Reset, or Have Deadlines (US, as of Oct 2026)

Scope note: ~19 web searches, no full-page fetches. Most figures come from search-result summaries of primary sources (Vanguard, Pew, IRS-derived law firm alerts, Carta, Compt). Where a number comes from a vendor or a secondary write-up, I say so. Statements that rest only on general domain knowledge, not on a source found in this session, are in "Inferences" and labeled "(unverified this session)".

## PTO: use-it-or-lose-it, rollover caps, floating holidays, sabbaticals, and how much Americans forfeit

### Takeaway
Roughly 46–67% of US workers leave some PTO unused, depending on the survey. The most-cited dollar figure (236M days, $65.5B fully forfeited) is from 2018 U.S. Travel data, and I found no newer U.S. Travel days-forfeited estimate. Forfeiture is legal in most states but banned in California (and reportedly CO, MT, NE). There, caps on accrual replace forfeiture and accrued vacation must be paid out at separation.

### Cited Findings
- 46% of US workers with paid time off take less than they're offered (Pew survey of 5,188 workers, Feb 6–12 2023, published Aug 2023). Upper-income 51%, middle 45%, lower 41%. Managers, salaried, and college-degree workers are more likely to leave time unused. Reasons: 52% didn't feel they needed it, 49% worried about falling behind, 43% felt bad about co-workers covering — [Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/08/10/more-than-4-in-10-u-s-workers-dont-take-all-their-paid-time-off/)
- About 5% of Pew respondents had unlimited PTO. 89% said PTO was extremely or very important to them — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2023/03/30/americans-pto-time-off-survey)
- U.S. Travel Association (with Oxford Economics/Ipsos), 2018 data: 768M vacation days unused (+9% vs 2017). 236M were forfeited completely, worth $65.5B in lost benefits — [Hotel-Online summary of U.S. Travel study](https://www.hotel-online.com/news/study-a-record-768-million-u-s-vacation-days-went-unused-in-18-opportunity-cost-in-the-billions)
- Project Time Off (U.S. Travel), 2016 data: 54% of employees ended the year with unused time, 662M days total — [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2017/05/23/vacation-time-americans-unused/)
- Solitaired (2025): 67% of American workers will leave some PTO unused in 2025. Pennsylvania had the highest average unused, 6.8 days — [MyChesCo](https://www.mychesco.com/a/lifestyle/pennsylvania-workers-leave-more-vacation-time-unused-than-any-state-in-2025/)
- FlexJobs (2025): 23% of US workers took no vacation days in the past year — [Trade & Industry Development](https://www.tradeandindustrydev.com/industry/all-industries/nearly-1-4-us-workers-took-no-pto-past-year-says-35094.md)
- Eagle Hill Consulting: 48% of US workers don't expect to take all their allotted vacation by calendar year-end — [SHRM](https://shrm.org/topics-tools/news/benefits-compensation/nearly-half-of-employees-expect-to-leave-vacation-time-unused--w)
- Sorbet (2023 data): 62% of workers with PTO did not use all of it, wasting about a third — [Nasdaq](https://nasdaq.com/articles/american-workers-are-letting-third-their-vacation-time-go-waste-report)
- A widely repeated "$312B unused PTO" figure has no verifiable underlying source; treat it as suspect — [Vacation Tracker](https://vacationtracker.io/blog/american-pto-usage-is-out-of-control/)
- California: use-it-or-lose-it is illegal because accrued vacation counts as earned wages. Accrual caps are allowed, commonly about 1.5–2× (often 1.75×) the annual accrual rate. Accrued vacation must be paid immediately at termination — [Nolo](https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/california-rules-vacation-paid-time-off.html); [SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/in/topics-tools/employment-law-compliance/nowhere-to-go-nothing-to-managing-vacation-time-california)
- California is reportedly one of the few states, with Colorado, Montana, and Nebraska, that forbids use-it-or-lose-it. Most states permit it or are silent. This list was not verified against statutes — [Shouse Law](https://www.shouselaw.com/ca/blog/use-it-or-lose-it-vacation-policy/)

### Inferences
- Deadline cadence for an extraction pipeline: forfeiture typically happens at calendar year-end (Dec 31), plan/fiscal year-end, or the hire anniversary. Rollover caps (e.g., "carry over up to 40 hrs," or "must use carryover by Mar 31") appear in the employee handbook / PTO policy, and the current balance appears on the pay stub or in the HRIS (Workday, ADP, Rippling, Gusto). (unverified this session)
- Dollar value: one forfeited day ≈ annual salary / 260. For a new grad at $80K, that's about $308/day; forfeiting 5 days ≈ $1,500.
- Floating holidays: usually 1–3 days, almost always use-it-or-lose-it by year-end, and often not paid out at separation, even in payout states, if the policy defines them as holidays. (unverified this session; no stats found)
- Sabbaticals: usually tied to tenure milestones (e.g., several weeks at 5 years). Often must be taken within a window (e.g., 12–24 months of eligibility) or they're lost. (unverified this session)
- Unlimited PTO: nothing to forfeit, but also nothing paid out at exit. Pew found only ~5% of workers have it.

### Gaps
- I found no 2024–2026 U.S. Travel Association estimate of days *forfeited* (vs unused). The 2018 figures are the latest found.
- No reliable stats on floating-holiday or sabbatical forfeiture rates.
- The state list for payout and forfeiture bans was not verified against statutes.

## Lifestyle spending accounts (LSAs), wellness stipends, L&D / tuition reimbursement, conference and book budgets

### Takeaway
LSAs and stipends usually reset annually (some monthly or quarterly) with no rollover. Vendor data (Compt) shows about $850/employee/yr on average and high (89%) utilization in all-inclusive LSAs. Tuition reimbursement is the most under-used perk: fewer than 10% of eligible workers use it, with a median participation around 3.75%. The tax-free limit is $5,250/yr under Section 127, made permanent and indexed after 2026 by OBBBA.

### Cited Findings
- Compt 2025 full-year benchmark: average stipend funding $850/employee/yr across all programs. Small companies (<100) $1,675, midsize $1,055, large (1,000+) $649. The median all-inclusive LSA is $1,200/yr. All-inclusive LSAs show 89% utilization (spend ÷ funds issued per cadence) and 93% participation. 64% of Compt customers offer an all-inclusive LSA (55% in 2024). This is vendor data from Compt's own customer base — [Compt LSA guide](https://compt.io/guide/lifestyle-spending-accounts-guide/); [Compt stipend benchmarks](https://compt.io/blog/stipend-and-lifestyle-spending-account-benchmarks/); [Compt 2026 benchmark report](https://compt.io/resources/2026-lifestyle-benefits-benchmark-report/)
- Compt midyear 2025 (Jan–Jun) reported $1,029/employee, which differs from the $850 full-year figure because the two use different bases — [Compt midyear 2025](https://compt.io/blog/compt-2025-midyear-benchmark-report-employee-stipend-funding/)
- IRC §127: the first $5,250/yr of employer educational assistance is excluded from income — [Cornell LII 26 USC 127](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/127)
- OBBBA made §127 permanent for payments after 2025. The limit stays at $5,250 for 2025–2026 and is COLA-indexed for tax years after 2026. Student-loan repayment is also covered — [PlanSponsor on IRS FAQ](https://www.plansponsor.com/irs-updates-faq-on-section-127-educational-assistance-programs/); [ACE](https://acenet.edu/Documents/TPs-Tax-Reform-2025-Section-127.pdf)
- 57% of organizations offer tuition assistance (SHRM 2024) — [PlanSponsor](https://www.plansponsor.com/irs-updates-faq-on-section-127-educational-assistance-programs/)
- Fewer than 10% of workers at companies offering tuition reimbursement use it (WSJ 2018, secondhand citation) — [MyShortlister](https://www.myshortlister.com/insights/tuition-reimbursement-statistics)
- Training magazine Top 125 applicants: mean tuition-reimbursement participation is 7.35% of employees, median 3.75%, range 0.1–95% — [Training magazine](https://trainingmag.com/?p=9753)

### Inferences
- LSAs and wellness, L&D, conference, and book stipends are typically employer-owned budgets, not employee money. Unspent funds revert at period end (monthly, quarterly, or annual reset), and claims usually have a submission deadline (e.g., 30–90 days after period end). LSAs are taxable income, unlike FSAs. (unverified this session)
- Tuition reimbursement often has a service-commitment clawback (repay if you leave within 12–24 months), pre-approval deadlines before the course starts, and grade requirements. Key fields to extract: annual cap, pre-approval rule, clawback period, and claim deadline. (unverified this session)
- Data sources for extraction: the benefits guide / total-rewards summary (amounts, cadence), the stipend vendor portal (Compt, Forma, Benepass, Pluto) for balance and reset date, the employee handbook (L&D and tuition policy), and the offer letter (sometimes lists the L&D or home-office budget).
- Rescue action: put a calendar reminder about 30 days before each reset, and submit receipts before the claim deadline.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) LSA utilization data. No Forma or Benepass public statistics found in this session.
- No current (2024–2026) tuition-reimbursement utilization statistics. No dollar estimate of total unused L&D budgets.
- No statistics on conference or book stipend usage.

## Commuter/transit, phone/internet/home-office, meal stipends, childcare benefits

### Takeaway
Pre-tax transit and parking limits are $340/month each in 2026. Unused commuter balances are forfeited at termination, with no cash refund, after a plan-specific run-out (often 60–90 days). Elections can be changed monthly, so the rescue is to right-size elections and spend down before leaving. The dependent-care FSA limit rose from $5,000 to $7,500 in 2026 (OBBBA), but only if the employer amended its plan.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 qualified transportation fringe limits (Rev. Proc. 2025-32): transit/vanpool $340/mo (from $325), qualified parking $340/mo (from $325) — [Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/irs-releases-annual-increases-to-health-fsa-and-transportation-fringe-benefit-limits-for-2026); [Mercer](https://www.mercer.com/en-us/insights/law-and-policy/mercer-projects-2026-transportation-and-health-fsa-limits/)
- The TCJA excluded bicycle commuting reimbursement for 2018–2025. Its 2026 status was not confirmed in the sources found — [Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/irs-releases-annual-increases-to-health-fsa-and-transportation-fringe-benefit-limits-for-2026)
- Terminated employees cannot use commuter funds after termination and the run-out period, and employers cannot cash-refund forfeited balances. The employer may keep forfeitures, use them for plan expenses, or reallocate them uniformly. Elections can be joined, changed, or revoked monthly — [Newfront](https://newfront.com/blog/commuter-benefits-upon-termination-employment-2)
- Example run-outs: a 60-day claims run-out after termination (one university plan), and a stored-value card active 90 days post-employment before the balance is forfeited — [UPenn FAQ](https://cms.business-services.upenn.edu/transportation/contact-us/faqs/commuter-faqs/35-commuter-passes/adp-commuter-check-card/360-i-have-taken-another-position-and-have-left-penn-how-do-i-claim-my-unused-credits.html)
- Dependent care FSA: $7,500 ($3,750 MFS) for tax years beginning on or after Jan 1, 2026 under OBBBA, up from $5,000, which had been the limit since 1986. Employers are not required to adopt the higher limit, and plan amendments were needed — [Polsinelli](https://www.polsinelli.com/publications/new-year-new-dependent-care-fsa-limits); [Hill Ward Henderson](https://www.hwhlaw.com/advisory-dependent-care-fsa-limit-increased-to-7-500-for-2026)

### Inferences
- Commuter max annual value: $340 × 12 = $4,080 of pre-tax transit plus $4,080 of parking. At a 30% combined marginal rate, that's about $1,200 per benefit per year in tax savings that a non-enrollee forgoes.
- Dependent care FSA is use-it-or-lose-it by plan year, possibly with a grace period of up to 2.5 months. Childcare at $7,500 × ~30% ≈ $2,250 in tax savings. (grace-period detail unverified this session; health FSA belongs to an adjacent research area)
- Phone, internet, home-office, and meal stipends are typically paid via payroll (taxable) or reimbursement with a monthly claim deadline. Unclaimed months usually don't roll over. Home-office one-time stipends often must be used within the first 30–90 days of hire. Look in the offer letter, onboarding docs, and the expense policy (Expensify, Navan). (unverified this session; no stats found)
- Extraction sources: pay stub (pre-tax commuter deduction line), commuter vendor portal (WageWorks/HealthEquity, Edenred, Commuter Benefit Solutions), and benefits guide (limits, run-out).

### Gaps
- No stats on commuter-benefit enrollment or forfeiture rates, or on phone, internet, or meal stipend usage.
- No primary IRS text was retrieved on the no-refund rule (it comes from a broker blog). An IRS letter allowing transit↔parking transfers is mentioned in one source but not verified.

## Retirement & equity: 401(k) match, true-up, vesting; ESPP windows and lookback; RSU/option post-termination exercise; HSA employer seed

### Takeaway
The biggest-dollar expiring items for young workers are: (1) the unclaimed 401(k) match (historically 1 in 4 participants missed some of it, worth about $1,336/yr each), (2) unvested employer contributions lost at separation (30% of separations involve forfeitures, averaging 40% of the departing participant's balance), (3) vested startup options lapsing under the typical 90-day post-termination exercise window (about 50,000 workers walked away from more than $1.8B in 2022, per Carta), and (4) skipped ESPPs (a 15% discount with lookback is now standard, yet most companies see less than 50% participation).

### Cited Findings
**401(k) match & participation**
- Vanguard How America Saves 2026 (2025 data): participation is 94% under auto-enrollment vs 64% under voluntary enrollment. 61% of plans use auto-enroll (79% of plans with 1,000+ participants). 62% of plans default at 4%+. Average deferral is 7.6% (median 6.6%). Total with employer contributions averages 12.1% (median 11.6%) — [NAPA](https://www.napa-net.org/news/2026/6/plan-design-drives-record-retirement-participation-better-outcomes-vanguard/); [Vanguard HAS 2026 PDF](https://workplace.vanguard.com/content/dam/inst/iig-transformation/insights/pdf/has/how-america-saves-2026.pdf); [PSCA](https://www.psca.org/news/psca-news/2026/6/automatic-enrollment-has-sent-participation-rates-soaring/)
- Vanguard match research (2023 recordkeeping data): only 22% of workers earning under $30K maximized their match, vs 80% of those earning $150K+. About 60% of match dollars went to the 43% contributing above the match cap — [Vanguard research summary](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/corporatesite/us/en/corp/articles/new-401k-match-formulas-can-unlock-retirement-gains.html); [Vanguard PDF](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/dam/corp/research/pdf/better_match_formulas_401k_plans.pdf)
- 86% of Vanguard plans offer matching contributions, and the median match value is 4% of pay (Motley Fool on HAS 2025, secondary) — [Webull/Motley Fool](https://www.webull.com/news/13201341490373632)
- Financial Engines "Missing Out" (2015; 4.4M participants, 553 companies): 1 in 4 participants miss some or all of the match, averaging $1,336/yr, about $24B/yr nationally. This likely understates the total because it excludes non-contributors. Younger and lower-income employees were the most likely to miss out. Compounded, the loss can reach $42,855 over 20 years — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2015/05/12/yees-missing-out-on-24b-of-free-money.html); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/retirement/workers-missing-out-24-billion-employer-matches-n357631)

**Vesting**
- Vanguard (Feb 2025; 4.7M separations, 1,500 plans): forfeitures occur in 30% of job separations, are most common among lower-income participants, and average 40% of the affected participants' final balances. Vesting recoups only about 2.5% of employer contributions and shows no retention effect. Only 33% of participants could correctly say whether their plan has a vesting schedule — [Vanguard article (via MN LCPR)](https://www.lcpr.mn.gov/vestingWG/7.28.26/Vanguard%20article%20does_401k_vesting_help_retain_workers%202.2025.pdf); [NAPA](https://www.napa-net.org/news/2025/2/do-401k-vesting-schedules-help-with-worker-retention/)
- 49% of Vanguard plans use immediate vesting (2024 HAS data); the rest use graded or cliff schedules — [NAPA](https://www.napa-net.org/news/2025/2/do-401k-vesting-schedules-help-with-worker-retention/)
- Forfeited balances may offset employer contributions, pay plan expenses, or be reallocated to remaining participants — [Rehmann](https://www.rehmann.com/resource/dont-take-forfeitures-for-granted/)

**Stock options / PTEP**
- The vast majority of startups give 90 days post-termination to exercise. 82% of companies had a median window of about 89–92 days. In 2022, just under 50,000 workers walked away from vested options worth more than $1.8B (409A basis) — [Carta](https://carta.com/data/linkedin-employee-stock-options-90-days-too-short/); [Carta](https://carta.com/data/linkedin-90-day-option-window-unfair/)
- 96% of option agreements on Carta have a PTEP of 90 days or less (older figure). In Q1 2024, only 20% of terminated grants had a PTEP over 90 days. ISOs lose ISO status if exercised more than 90 days after termination — [Carta PTEP](https://carta.com/learn/equity/leaving-company/post-termination-exercise-period/)

**ESPP**
- NASPP/Deloitte 2023: 85% of qualified ESPPs offer a 15% discount (70% in 2020), and 83% offer a lookback (62% in 2020). 65% of companies report that less than half of employees participate. Only 19% see participation above 70% — [NASPP](https://www.naspp.com/blog/five-trends-in-espps); [NCEO](https://nceo.org/blog/naspp-survey-shows-espps-somewhat-more-conservative)
- Fidelity/Radford 2016 (secondhand): participation is 39% with a lookback vs 21% without, and 36% with a 15% discount vs 15% with a 5% discount — [Cooley deck](https://www.cooley.com/~/media/cooley/event-material/comp-talks-espp-design-and-administration-november-2017.ashx)

**HSA employer seed**
- EBRI (2024 data): among contributing accountholders, the average employer HSA contribution was $727 (down slightly), and the average employee contribution was $2,308 — [PlanAdviser/EBRI](https://www.planadviser.com/new-milestone-majority-of-hsa-assets-invested/)
- Devenir year-end 2025: about $60B contributed, about $45B withdrawn in 2025 — [Devenir 2025 YE summary](https://www.devenir.com/wp-content/uploads/2025-Year-End-Devenir-HSA-Research-Report-Executive-Summary.pdf)

### Inferences
- 401(k) true-up: if the match is computed per paycheck without an annual true-up, front-loading (hitting the IRS deferral max early) forfeits match for the remaining pay periods. The plan document or SPD says whether a true-up exists. Rescue: spread contributions across the year, or confirm the true-up. (unverified this session; no stats found)
- Vesting cliff: a common 3-year cliff (or 2–6 year graded) schedule means leaving at 2 yrs 11 mo loses 100% of the match. The vesting date appears in the SPD and on the recordkeeper portal (Fidelity, Vanguard, Empower), which shows a "vested balance". A new grad leaving after 2 years with a 4% match on $80K could forfeit about $6,400 plus growth.
- Many 401(k) plans require being employed on Dec 31 or completing 1,000 hours to receive that year's match or profit-sharing contribution ("last day rule"). (unverified this session)
- ESPP: enrollment usually opens only before each offering period, typically every 6 months. Missing the window means waiting about 6 months. A 15% discount with a lookback implies a guaranteed ≥17.6% gain on contributions if shares are sold immediately. The IRS caps purchases at $25K of stock value per year. (unverified this session)
- RSUs: unvested RSUs are forfeited at separation. A departure timed just before a vest date (often quarterly, with a 1-year cliff for new hires) loses that tranche. Look in the grant agreement and the equity portal (Carta, Shareworks, E*TRADE, Schwab). (unverified this session)
- HSA employer seed is often deposited only if you enroll in the HDHP and open or activate the HSA (sometimes after completing a wellness task). The money, once deposited, is the employee's and does not expire, but an unclaimed seed is lost. (unverified this session)

### Gaps
- No post-2015 national "missed match" dollar estimate was found. The HAS 2026 full-text match-maximization rate was not retrieved.
- No data on the prevalence of true-up provisions.
- No current ESPP participation stats for 2024–2026 and no dollar estimate of forgone ESPP gains.
- No Devenir employer-seed average was found; EBRI's $727 is the best figure.

## Referral bonuses, sign-on/relocation clawbacks, annual open enrollment, 30-day life-event window, COBRA 60-day election

### Takeaway
Sign-on and relocation bonuses typically carry a 12-month (sometimes 24-month) clawback, ideally prorated monthly, though cliff clauses exist. COBRA gives 60 days to elect after notice, 45 more days to pay the first premium, and a 30-day grace period for later premiums. Life-event and open-enrollment windows are short and strict.

### Cited Findings
- SEC-filed agreements show 12-month repayment of sign-on and relocation amounts, prorated monthly (e.g., 92% owed in month 1 declining to 4% in month 12). Some clawbacks trigger only on voluntary resignation or termination for cause — [Nabi agreement, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000072444/000119312506159361/dex104.htm); [HMS Holdings, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001196501/000104746915004227/a2224512zex-10_48.htm); [Aetna offer letter, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001122304/000095012300009505/y40132ex10-20.txt)
- Non-prorated cliff clauses exist: one physician who left in month 11 of 12 owed the full bonus — [ResidencyAdvisor](https://residencyadvisor.com/resources/physician-contract-negotiation/7-clawback-terms-physicians-must-fix-before-signing-a-job-contract)
- COBRA: 60 days to elect after the qualifying event/notice, then 45 days after election to pay the first premium. Later premiums have a 30-day grace period. COVID-era tolling (Outbreak Period) has since expired — [SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/ResourcesAndTools/hr-topics/benefits/Pages/COBRA-election-and-payment-periods.aspx); [Ogletree](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/irs-clarifies-application-of-deadline-extensions-to-cobra)

### Inferences
- HIPAA special enrollment: generally 30 days from a qualifying life event (marriage, birth/adoption, loss of other coverage) to add or change group health coverage, and 60 days for Medicaid/CHIP-related events. Missing it locks you in until the next open enrollment. (unverified this session; no DOL page retrieved)
- Annual open enrollment: usually 2–4 weeks in Oct–Nov for a Jan 1 plan year. Elections often default to the prior year, but FSA, HSA, commuter, and ESPP elections often do NOT roll over and must be re-elected. Look in the HRIS benefits portal (Workday, ADP, Benefitfocus, bswift) and the open-enrollment email/guide. (unverified this session)
- Turning 26: young adults lose parental coverage at 26 and face the same 60-day COBRA / special-enrollment clocks, which is a key trigger for the new-grad audience. (unverified this session)
- Referral bonuses: often require the referred hire to stay 60–90 days, and the referrer must still be employed at payout. They're typically $1K–$5K+ in tech. Look in the internal referral policy or ATS (Greenhouse, Lever). (unverified this session; no stats found)
- Extraction sources: the offer letter or signed repayment agreement (clawback amount, period, proration, triggers), the COBRA election notice (mailed by the administrator, with deadline dates), and the benefits guide / SPD (life-event windows).

### Gaps
- No stats on how often sign-on clawbacks are enforced, or the typical sign-on amount for new grads.
- No referral-bonus statistics.
- No stats on missed COBRA elections or missed special-enrollment windows.
- HIPAA special enrollment rules not verified from a DOL primary source in this session.
